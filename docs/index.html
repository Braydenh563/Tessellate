Your notes, pieced together · offline notebook · Windows, Linux, macOS from source

Capture anything. Ask anything.

Tessellate files it, links it and answers from it, all on your own device: a local AI files each thought, tags it and links it to what you already wrote. Ask a question and the answer cites the notes it came from. All of it runs on your computer.

Three steps, and you only do two of them.

Most notebooks leave the filing to you. Tessellate reads each note as you save it and works out where it belongs.

  1. 1

    Capture

    Type, paste, draw, dictate or import. Alt+N opens a quick note over any tab, and voice is transcribed on your machine.

    Quick noteCtrl+Enter to save

    Book the Alfa Pendular tickets before the price goes up #portugal

  2. 2

    It files itself

    A local model picks the category by meaning, suggests tags and links the notes it relates to, and says how sure it is. Keep each with one press.

    Category
    Travel
    Tags
    #trains#portugal
    Linked to
    Portugal trip, overview
    Certainty
    Fairly sure
  3. 3

    Ask, with sources

    Ask in plain words. Each sentence of the answer cites the note behind it, so nothing has to be taken on trust.

    What do I still need to book for Portugal?

    The Alfa Pendular tickets1 and the hire car in Porto2.

    • 1Trains
    • 2Hire car for the Douro valley

Everything else a thinking notebook needs.

A long-form editor, boards and mind maps, a graph, a timeline and an assistant that can act for you. All of it offline.

Notes

Write in fragments, find it organised.

A loose thought lands next to its neighbours: filed into a category, tagged, and linked to what it relates to.

  • Rename, merge, split and move categories, each step undoable
  • Pins, threads, attachments, revision history and a recycle bin
  • Search that understands tags, exact phrases and exclusions

Chat and agent mode

A conversation that can do the work.

Chats are saved and resumable. Turn on agent mode and Atlas searches, links, organises and sets reminders, showing every step.

  • Anything destructive asks first
  • Skills run multi-step jobs as a visible checklist, and you can write your own
  • A small-model mode keeps small models on task, one step at a time

Graph

See how your thinking connects.

Your notes drawn as a map, coloured by category. Clusters, loose webs and stray notes show at once, and every link says why it exists.

  • Drop one note onto another to link them
  • Trace the chain of links between any two notes
  • Force, tree, radial and arc layouts

Documents

Long-form writing, with checks that stay local.

A real editor for what outgrows a note, with Live, Source, Split and Read views. Spelling and grammar are checked on your computer.

  • Version history, focus mode, and code files with line numbers
  • The Writing room turns rough thoughts into a proper note
  • Export as Markdown, HTML, PDF or Word

Boards and mind maps

Think on a canvas.

Cards, sketches, images and shapes that hold text, joined by labelled connectors. Turn a board into a mind map whose topics can be tasks or notes.

  • Shapes, flowchart symbols and icons
  • Mind maps export as Markdown, OPML or FreeMind, and import XMind
  • Every board is searchable like a note

And around it.

Every note and reminder on one axis, placed by the date a note talks about.

Reminders

Type “call Sam tomorrow evening” and it is scheduled.

Library

Notes, documents, chats, boards, files and bookmarks in one place.

Popup agent

Ctrl+Shift+A asks Atlas to capture, find or remind without leaving the page.

Atlas

The star spirit behind the filing and the answers. Keep it in the corner, or switch it off.

Focus mode

The page and nothing else, with writing checks when you want them.

Light or dark

Every theme in both, your own accent, type, density and corners.

  • ImportMarkdown, PDF, Word and slide decks, plus Notion, Obsidian, Evernote and Apple Notes.
  • Read imagesA vision model or local OCR, with each region checked before it becomes a note.
  • Voice and meetingsRecordings transcribed on your machine.

Nothing leaves your computer unless you send it.

There is no service behind Tessellate. The app, the AI and your notes all run on your machine. Here is everything that could reach the network, and where it stands.

Install in a few minutes.

Only running from source needs a terminal, and the launcher does the setup for you.

Windows

  1. Download Tessellate-Setup-*.exe from the latest release.
  2. Run it. The app opens in its own window.

The installer is not code-signed yet, so Windows may show a “protected your PC” screen. The guide shows what to click.

Windows installer guide

Linux

  1. Download Tessellate-*-linux-x86_64.tar.gz from the same page.
  2. Unpack it and run Tessellate.
tar -xzf Tessellate-*-linux-x86_64.tar.gz

The window needs GTK and WebKit, which most desktops already have.

Linux package guide

macOS, or from source

  1. Clone the repository and run the start script.
  2. It builds a private Python environment and opens the app.
git clone https://github.com/Braydenh563/Tessellate.git
cd Tessellate
./start-desktop.sh

Use ./start.sh for a browser tab, or add --doctor to check the machine and print a fix for each problem.

Step-by-step install guide

Then add a model, or don’t.

The notebook works without one. Filing and answers switch on when you add a model, and you can change it any time in Settings, Models.

Questions people ask.

Something else? Open an issue on GitHub.

Is it free?

Yes. Tessellate is free and open source under the GNU Affero General Public License. You may use, study, change and share it, and anything built on it stays under the same licence.

Do I need a powerful computer or a GPU?

No. The app is light, and small models run on an ordinary laptop with no GPU. A bigger model gives better answers if your machine can hold one; the model guide suggests one for each size of machine.

Does it work without any AI?

Yes. With no model, notes are filed as Uncategorised, tags are suggested from similar notes, search uses full-text matching with stemming and spelling correction, and documents, boards, mind maps, the graph, the timeline and reminders all keep working.

Where are my notes stored?

In one folder on your computer: a single SQLite file for your notes, your uploads, your preferences and a daily backup. Move the folder anywhere, and export everything as JSON, CSV or Markdown, or as a password-sealed backup.

Does anything go online?

Only what you switch on. Web search and the update check are off by default, a model address that is not local is refused unless you allow it, and Settings, Privacy lists every connection the app has made. There is no telemetry.

Can I use it on my phone?

Yes, from your own network. The server listens on your computer only until you switch on network access in Settings, Account & security. A phone then opens it in its browser, behind your password and over HTTPS.

Can I bring my notes from another app?

Yes. It imports folders of Markdown, PDF, Word and slide decks, and exports from Notion, Obsidian, Evernote and Apple Notes. Importing the same thing twice adds only what is new.

Why does Windows warn me about the installer?

The installer is not code-signed yet, so Windows SmartScreen shows a “protected your PC” screen the first time. The install guide shows exactly what to click, and the source for every build is on GitHub.

How do I report a bug or a security problem?

Open an issue on GitHub for bugs and ideas. For a vulnerability, follow the security policy rather than opening a public issue.
